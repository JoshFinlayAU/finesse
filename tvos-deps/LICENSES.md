## Questions for the owner

1. **Replacing the libraries.** LGPL 2.1 section 6 is commonly met with a shared library mechanism that lets a user swap in a modified library. These frameworks are dynamic, which is what MPVKit's static link could not offer. The catch is that on an Apple TV an app's bundle is signed, and an App Store copy can't be altered and re-signed by its user. So dynamic linking removes the static-link problem without proving that a user can really make the swap on a device. This is the main open question.
2. **Apache 2.0 with LGPL 2.1.** FFmpeg's `LICENSE.md` says Apache 2.0 is incompatible with LGPL 2.1 and needs `--enable-version3` to combine with FFmpeg. Our FFmpeg links nothing under Apache. mpv and libplacebo, both LGPL 2.1 or later, do link MoltenVK, which is Apache 2.0, as a separate dynamic library. Both are "or later", which may help. MPVKit has the same pairing, plus OpenSSL. It needs a legal reading.
3. **glslang's parser.** The Bison exception lets a parser generated by Bison be part of a larger work under any terms. The compiled parser is in Libplacebo. It looks fine and is here so nobody finds it by surprise.
4. **mpv's grant.** Upstream's statement quoted above means `-Dgpl=false` is a convenience rather than a promise. The `Copyright` file lists which files are GPL-only, and the build leaves them out.
5. **Patents.** Copyright licences say nothing about patents on codecs such as HEVC, DTS, TrueHD and Dolby. FFmpeg's decoders for these are included.

## What we'd have to ship, as we read it

- **Notices:** the full text of LGPL 2.1, plus each permissive licence and copyright notice above, visible to the user. An in-app licences screen is the usual place, and the app doesn't have one yet.
- **FreeType's credit:** "Portions of this software are copyright (c) <year> The FreeType Project (www.freetype.org). All rights reserved."
- **Apache 2.0:** the licence text, plus any `NOTICE` file from MoltenVK or SPIRV-Cross. None was checked for yet.
- **Source:** for each LGPL component, the exact source and our patches. Either ship it alongside or include a written offer valid for three years. Hosting it next to each app release from a server we control is the simple route. `versions.sh`, `checksums.sha256`, `patches/` and these scripts are what someone needs to rebuild.
- **How to replace them:** written instructions for building a replacement framework with these scripts and putting it in the app. See question 1 for how far that really works.
- **A statement in the app** that it uses these libraries and that they are under the LGPL.
- **Our own licence choice:** the app's licence is undecided, and LGPL section 6 also limits terms that forbid reverse engineering for debugging such changes. That belongs in whatever terms the app ships with.
